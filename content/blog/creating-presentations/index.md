Around the same time in **mid-April**, between regular classes and the cybersecurity selection paperwork, I refreshed how I think about **presentation design** — not “more slides,” but clearer thinking on screen.

## Start from the outcome

Before opening PowerPoint, LibreOffice **Impress**, or **Google Slides**, I write a **one-sentence goal**: after ten minutes, what should the listener *believe* or *be able to do*? Everything else is scaffolding: an **outline** with three to five moves, each with one memorable visual or example.

## Slides are subtitles, not a script

I keep **one idea per slide**, avoid walls of text, and use **large type** with generous margins. Diagrams beat bullet lists when the concept is spatial or procedural. If I need detail, it belongs in a **handout** or appendix link — not read aloud line by line.

## Rehearsal beats decoration

Transitions and stock photos rarely rescue a weak story. I rehearse **out loud** with a timer, click through once in “presenter” view, and fix anything that forces me to apologise (“this is a bit small…”). That pass usually cuts twenty percent of the deck and improves pacing more than any theme.
