The second week of **April** was dominated by one big extracurricular thread: **selection for an educational cybersecurity shift** (a short residential programme built around lectures, labs, and team exercises on secure systems, threat models, and responsible disclosure).

## What happened

I submitted the **application pack** (motivation, prior coursework, and a short skills questionnaire), then sat the **first-stage screening** — a timed online module mixing **MCQs** on networking basics, simple **risk scenarios**, and a few **logic puzzles** meant to test careful reading under pressure. A few days later the organisers confirmed I had **cleared stage one** and would receive details for the next step (interview / practical slot — the exact format is still TBA).

## How it felt

Honestly, I was more nervous about **time limits** than about the security content itself — but preparing forced me to tighten gaps in **TCP/IP vocabulary**, **password hygiene**, and **what “attack surface” really means** in a small system. Whatever happens next, the revision alone was worthwhile.
